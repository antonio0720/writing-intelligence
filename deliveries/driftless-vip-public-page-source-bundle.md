## Mission

Give a stranger a clear path from the work that is stuck, to the result they want, to one next step. The public site must sell neither AI activity nor a borrowed compliance label. It must make one promise: Driftless VIP works on a defined business result inside the authority the customer grants, and the customer can see what happened.

## Source and Claim Boundary

This bundle is grounded in the supplied operating directive and the current Driftless VIP public-content source observed on 2026-09-30. It deliberately removes public claims that the source package does not support.

| Keep or say | Do not say without separate current proof |
|---|---|
| A customer defines the desired result, boundaries, and evidence before work starts. | A fixed plan price, a capacity limit, a savings estimate, or a provider price. |
| A result can be priced and settled by the kind of work completed, rather than by model attempts. | Generic FedRAMP, GovCloud, ATO, SOC, ISO, HIPAA, BAA, PCI, air-gap, or certification status. |

The commercial copy below uses **Fear → Emotion → Logic** and **Hook → Story → Offer** without exposing those internal names. It is customer language, not architecture language.

## 1. Homepage — `/`

### Hero

**Get the work that matters moving.**

Your business does not have a shortage of software. It has work that waits: leads that cool off, handoffs that disappear, approvals that sit, customers who do not hear back, and good ideas that never reach the market.

Tell Driftless VIP what must become true. Connect the people and systems involved. Set the boundaries. Driftless VIP finds what is holding the work up, does the work you authorize, and shows you what changed.

**Primary action:** Put Driftless VIP to work → `/put-to-work`
**Secondary action:** Build your business → `/signup`
**Enterprise action:** Start with one enterprise or mission workflow → `/enterprise-evidence`

### The promise

You remain in charge of the business. You decide what the system may do on its own, what needs your approval, and what it must never do.

Driftless VIP is built to close the distance between the result you need and the reality you have now. It connects the work, finds the constraint, takes the next permitted step, checks the result, and keeps going only when the evidence says it should.

### The offer

**Do not pay for a machine trying. Pay for a result it agreed to finish.**

Before a result-based job starts, the agreement can name the work, the price, what will prove completion, what depends on someone else, and what happens if the work cannot be proven. A draft, a sent message, a confirmed delivery, and revenue are different results. We say which one we are responsible for before the work begins.

If Driftless VIP fails a result it accepted, the failed attempt is our cost. If the result cannot be established inside the agreed review window, the customer should not pay for uncertainty.

### Start small. Prove it. Expand only after it earns the right.

One owner can start with a stalled follow-up. A team can start with one broken handoff. An enterprise can start with one queue, one department, or one customer workflow. A mission organization can start with one bounded process.

The first job should be important enough to matter and narrow enough to prove. When the proof is real, expand from evidence—not enthusiasm.

## 2. Enterprise Evidence — `/enterprise-evidence`

### Hero

**Your reputation is on the line when a new system touches real work.**

Do not approve Driftless VIP because a demonstration looked good. Approve it only after you can answer what it will do, who may authorize it, what it may touch, what proves it worked, and what happens when it does not.

### The decision your team should be able to defend

Start with one business result: shorten a customer handoff, clear a queue, complete onboarding, operate a campaign inside its rules, or improve one mission process.

Then ask five plain questions:

1. What result are we hiring Driftless VIP to help produce?
2. What may it do without asking, what needs approval, and what is forbidden?
3. Which people, systems, and customer information are involved?
4. What evidence will show that the work happened and the result changed?
5. What happens if the system is uncertain, a provider fails, or the customer needs to leave?

The answers should be specific to the first workflow—not a deck of promises that asks your security, legal, operations, and finance teams to connect the dots themselves.

### What we bring to the review

We will show the evidence relevant to the work under consideration: the operating boundary, the approval path, the record of important actions, the result definition, the current control evidence appropriate to the review, and the exit path that applies to the proposed scope.

We will not ask you to treat architecture as authorization, a future possibility as a present control, or a sales claim as proof.

**Action:** Request an evidence review for one workflow → `/enterprise-evidence#request`

## 3. Security — `/security`

### Hero

**If the system can act, permission has to mean something.**

Security is not a badge in a footer. It is the practical answer to a customer’s question: who can do what, with which information, for which purpose, and what record remains afterward?

### The boundary a customer should understand

Your connected business accounts remain yours. When you use a supported bring-your-own-key connection, your underlying provider charge remains separate from Driftless VIP’s platform charge. Access to operate a workflow is not automatic permission to reuse its information for another purpose.

The system should not take an action simply because an AI can describe how to take it. Important work should stay inside the authority, approval, customer boundary, and purpose that apply to that work.

### Evidence, not a security performance

Ask us about the exact concern: access, approvals, data handling, connected accounts, an action record, continuity, or exit. We will provide the evidence appropriate to that question and proposed scope, while protecting details that would make the system easier to attack.

We will also tell you when a proposed use needs more proof before it should receive operational authority.

**Action:** Ask a security question about your workflow → `/security#request`

## 4. Government and Mission Work — `/government`

### Hero

**Start with one mission whose outcome matters.**

The risk is not simply that an AI gives a poor answer. The risk is that a system acts outside the authority, information boundary, deployment conditions, or proof requirements that the mission demands.

### A capability is not an authorization

Start with one defined responsibility: a service queue, case process, communication workflow, grant process, field operation, contractor handoff, or back-office task. Name the desired result, the people who may make decisions, the information the system may use, the actions it may take, and the evidence the program must retain.

Then decide whether the proposed environment, controls, contract, and applicable requirements are sufficient for that work. A system designed for a demanding environment is not automatically authorized for every mission. We will not sell an acronym where a requirements review is needed.

### The right first offer

Begin with a bounded mission review. We will map the first workflow, its authority, its information boundaries, its evidence needs, its continuity needs, and the questions that must be answered before operational authority expands.

**Action:** Start a mission requirements review → `/government#request`

## 5. Pricing — `/pricing`

### Hero

**Pay for the result—not the machine spinning its wheels.**

You should not pay more because a model took longer, retried more often, or failed halfway through. Driftless VIP prices the work it agrees to do and states what completion means before you authorize it.

### What every result-based price answers

Before you say yes, you should know:

1. **What work are we buying?** A usable artifact, an action, a system change, an outside confirmation, or a measurable business result are not the same thing.
2. **What proves it is complete?** The proof fits the promise. A saved page is not a sent campaign. A sent campaign is not a confirmed delivery. A confirmed delivery is not revenue.
3. **What is Driftless VIP responsible for?** The agreement names the boundary instead of pretending the platform controls what it cannot control.
4. **What happens if it fails or cannot be proven?** Platform failure and unresolved proof do not become a customer charge.

### Three ways to buy Driftless VIP

| When you need | The commercial relationship | What is clear before you commit |
|---|---|---|
| A defined piece of self-service work | **Result-settled SIGNAL usage** | The exact result, its price, its proof, its review window, and the settlement rule. |
| A business operating environment | **A recurring platform relationship** | The operating scope, platform services, governance, standard operations, and commercial terms. |
| A higher-stakes business result | **A verified outcome engagement** | The measurable target, responsibility boundary, evidence and attribution model, plus any base or outcome-linked fee. |

### What we do not sell

We do not sell minutes, model attempts, or hidden token markups as though they were customer outcomes. Compute is our operating cost. When you supply a supported AI-provider credential, that provider’s charge stays visible and separate from the platform charge.

Where Driftless VIP offers a free or deterministic path, that path should remain useful; a free entry point is not supposed to be made useless just to force an upgrade.

### The next step

Bring one result you need: recover a handoff, create a campaign, launch a page, clear a queue, book qualified consultations, or improve a measurable operating result. We will define the promise, the proof, and the price before the work begins.

**Primary action:** Name the result you need → `/put-to-work`
**Enterprise action:** Discuss a platform or outcome engagement → `/enterprise-evidence`
