# The 60 New Arc and Ending Requirements

Status: mixed **specified** and **locally executable subset**. See `arc_120_requirements.json` for current scope.

### N061 — Conscious External Want Contract

Record the concrete outcome the character knowingly seeks, its owner, earliest statement, and whether its pursuit drives external plot. [Notes pp.1–2]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N062 — Latent Internal Need Contract

Record the proposed inner development, why it matters, when/if the character recognizes it, and who is interpreting it. [Notes pp.1,5]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N063 — Want/Need Version History

A want may be replaced, reprioritized, attained, abandoned, or become a need; preserve prior states instead of overwriting history. [Notes pp.2,4,6]

**Scope:** partially_executable. revision chain, knowledge-time and as-of selection

### N064 — Outcome Quadrant Calculator

Provide sweet, semi-sweet, bittersweet, and bitter as a transparent 2×2 **view** of want/need outcomes, not an exhaustive theory of narrative endings. [Notes pp.1,3,5,7]

**Scope:** partially_executable. conservative 2x2 ending projection

### N065 — Nonbinary Outcome Vocabulary

Support fulfilled, partially fulfilled, relinquished, sacrificed, thwarted, unknown, disputed, and not-applicable rather than forcing every ending to yes/no.

**Scope:** partially_executable. nonbinary outcome preservation

### N066 — Want Versus Need Collision Model

Track scenes in which attaining the visible goal compromises inner need, or satisfying the need requires giving up the goal. [Notes pp.4,6–9]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N067 — Multiple Simultaneous Wants

Support competing goals at different levels, including immediate scene objectives, act-level targets, and a changed central goal. [Notes pp.2,4,6]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N068 — Need Interpretation Provenance

Treat 'this character needs X' as author-declared craft interpretation, not an objectively discoverable truth about a person.

**Scope:** partially_executable. author-asserted versus inferred provenance

### N069 — Multi-Protagonist Ending Matrix

Allow different characters to occupy different outcome quadrants; avoid requiring one universal ending label.

**Scope:** partially_executable. per-character ending focus refs

### N070 — Ending Valence Independence

Represent hope, dread, irony, moral cost, justice, closure, and audience feeling separately from want/need status.

**Scope:** partially_executable. closure, valence separation

### N071 — Character Ghost With Causal Evidence

Represent formative wounds, betrayals, losses, and experiences as possible causes of present defenses without assuming all behavior arises from trauma. [Notes pp.5–7]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N072 — Misbelief or Lie Representation

State the distorted rule a character treats as true and how it predicts their choices. [Notes pp.7–9]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N073 — Truth Counterposition Registry

Record competing values or perspectives that challenge the lie; do not declare one interpretation morally absolute by automation.

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N074 — Valued-Good Inventory

Identify something meaningful the character already possesses but undervalues, especially when a negative arc endangers it. [Notes pp.7–9]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N075 — Characteristic Moment Plant

Plant a telling early behavior that demonstrates temperament, capability, contradiction, or vulnerability. [Notes pp.3–4]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N076 — Foreshadowed Potential Test

A corruption, redemption, or major reversal should be causally legible from early evidence without becoming predictable. [Notes pp.3–4]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N077 — Crisis of Belief Engine

Track the moment a character's action collides with the belief that guided earlier actions.

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N078 — Revelation Versus Transformation Distinction

A character recognizing a truth is not evidence of lasting change; require later behavioral consequence.

**Scope:** partially_executable. choice reference review signal

### N079 — Choice-as-Proof Rule

Evaluate whether the climactic decision demonstrates adoption, rejection, or ambiguity of a belief instead of relying only on dialogue.

**Scope:** partially_executable. witnessed choice review signal

### N080 — Truth-Rejection Escalation

In negative arcs, track each credible opportunity to change and the character's chosen refusal, with accumulating harm. [Notes pp.7–9]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N081 — Positive Change Arc Specification

Model flaw/defense, catalytic pressure, competing truth, costly actions, new behavior, and its consequences. [Notes pp.5–7]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N082 — Flat Arc Specification

Allow the protagonist's core conviction to remain stable while their influence transforms other characters or the environment. [Notes p.2]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N083 — Corruption Arc Specification

Distinguish gradual embrace of morally destructive choices from sudden arbitrary villainy. [Notes pp.3–5]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N084 — Fall Arc Specification

Track the repeated failure to confront a central misconception and the good that is progressively sacrificed. [Notes pp.7–9]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N085 — Disillusionment and Open Arc Support

Represent arcs ending in wiser pessimism, unchanged belief, unresolved identity, or deliberate anti-transformation.

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N086 — Arc-Type Versus Ending-Type Independence

Never infer positive/flat/negative trajectory solely from sweet/semi-sweet/bitter outcome, or vice versa.

**Scope:** partially_executable. family independent of ending

### N087 — Impact Character Graph

Track the character whose value, choices, example, or confrontation pressures the protagonist to reconsider. [Notes p.6]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N088 — Truth-Bearer and Lie-Bearer Roles

Represent people who embody competing models of a meaningful life without flattening them into moral instruction devices. [Notes pp.8–9]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N089 — Transformation of Secondary Characters

For flat protagonists, explicitly test whether meaningful change occurs in supporting people or institutions. [Notes p.2]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N090 — Character Agency Across Arc

Every major arc transition must identify what the character chose, resisted, or suffered; distinguish forced change from chosen change.

**Scope:** partially_executable. separate character arc families

### N091 — Page-One Ending Seed

Trace the eventual outcome to early desire, belief, flaw, resource, promise, or conflict, rather than retrofitting a finale. [Notes p.2]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N092 — Catalyst and First Threshold

Record the event that makes the established want harder, reveals a new want, or forces commitment.

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N093 — Midpoint Revaluation

Support mid-story reinterpretation of the want, the lie, relationships, identity, or the nature of victory without hard-coding a 50% timestamp. [Notes pp.7–8]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N094 — Want Replacement Causal Chain

Verify that a new central goal grows from events and does not erase the significance of the original goal. [Notes pp.2,4,6]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N095 — Incremental Moral Thresholds

Show small choices making larger choices increasingly plausible, particularly in corruption arcs. [Notes pp.3–5]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N096 — Genuine Alternatives Before Sacrifice

A final sacrifice matters more if the easier or desired alternative was genuinely attainable at a cost.

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N097 — Minor Defeat and Reversal Chain

Make positive endings earned through real resistance, loss, recovery, and altered tactics rather than effortless reward. [Notes pp.2–3]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N098 — Character's Shadow Cost Accounting

Track whose needs, safety, relationships, or possibilities are harmed by pursuit of the protagonist's desire. [Notes pp.3–5,7–9]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N099 — Turning-Point Counterfactual

Ask whether removing a stated turning point changes later decisions; if not, the claimed turning point may be decorative.

**Scope:** partially_executable. dependency graph walk and cycle refusal

### N100 — Final State Versus Starting State Diff

Produce an evidence-linked comparison of the character's beliefs, relationships, conduct, wants, and losses at opening and closing scenes. [Notes pp.7–9]

**Scope:** partially_executable. state comparison through as-of queries

### N101 — Earned Sweet Ending Test

Require meaningful adversity, loss risk, and causally earned reconciliation before allowing both goal and need to be satisfied. [Notes pp.2–3]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N102 — Semi-Sweet Renunciation Test

Verify that loss or sacrifice of a want materially expresses fulfilled inner need, rather than random deprivation. [Notes pp.5–7]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N103 — Bittersweet External Victory Test

Distinguish visible external success from interior emptiness, broken relationships, or moral defeat. [Notes pp.7–9]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N104 — Bitter Ending Integrity

Make loss of both want and need causally connected to decisions or constraints, not imposed for artificial bleakness. [Notes pp.3–5]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N105 — Tragic Satisfaction Paradox

Allow readers to feel triumph, sympathy, dread, or admiration even when the protagonist's moral and personal trajectory is devastating. [Notes pp.3–5]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N106 — Catharsis Through Consequence

Ground emotional release in irreversible action, changed relationship, recognition, or loss rather than declarative speeches. [Notes pp.1,5–7]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N107 — Ending Irony Composer

Support outcomes where attaining the stated instrument destroys the underlying value, such as connection versus status. [Notes pp.8–9]

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N108 — Ending Echo With Difference

Return to an opening object, choice, gesture, relationship, or claim whose meaning has changed by the finale.

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N109 — Last-Scene Character Proof

Use observed conduct and changed circumstances to demonstrate final arc state without narrator explanation. [Notes pp.7–9]

**Scope:** partially_executable. closed ending event witness

### N110 — Reader Response Versus Author Intent

Measure readers' perceived satisfaction/ambiguity separately from the writer's intended arc or ending quadrant.

**Scope:** partially_executable. reader-valence inference review flag

### N111 — Canonical Arc Nodes, Not a Second Database

Extend existing `fictional_canon` and storyworld graph semantics with `narrative.want`, `narrative.need`, `narrative.belief`, `narrative.ghost`, `narrative.arc_turn`, and `narrative.ending` as typed nodes or compatible extension fields.

**Scope:** partially_executable. read-only projection shape, without canon writes

### N112 — State-Time-Aware Arc Transitions

Attach `valid_from`, `valid_until`, `known_at`, provenance, character, and causing scene/event to changes in desire and belief; preserve decision-time meaning.

**Scope:** partially_executable. valid-world and knowledge time preservation

### N113 — Two-Layer Tests: Deterministic and Interpretive

Test IDs, continuity, dependencies, transitions, and matrix projections deterministically; send psychological credibility and catharsis to qualified review.

**Scope:** partially_executable. typed deterministic validation

### N114 — Fiction-Safe Proof Vocabulary

State `consistent_with_canon`, `author_asserted`, `reviewer_inferred`, `reader_reported`, or `unverified`; never call aesthetic excellence machine-proven.

**Scope:** partially_executable. epistemic statuses preserved in reports

### N115 — Adversarial Fiction Benchmark Set

Test flat arcs, positive and negative arcs, partial outcomes, changed wants, multi-protagonist conflicts, anti-formula stories, and deceptively polished but emotionally hollow endings.

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N116 — Negative Controls Against Template Tyranny

The system must not automatically fail intentional nonlinear arcs, tragedy, unchanged protagonists, ambiguous endings, or endings without a single revelation.

**Scope:** partially_executable. anti-formula negative controls

### N117 — Source Provenance & Fair-Use Discipline

Preserve origins and rights status for the notes' film examples and named theorist; create new explanatory wording and original test fiction rather than reproducing protected scripts.

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N118 — Writer Override With Rationale

Permit author-authorized deviations from recommended craft patterns without silently altering canonical facts; preserve reasons, scope, and version history.

**Scope:** partially_executable. override specification and non-mutating policy

### N119 — Independent Reader Calibration

Compare automated findings with blinded human readers, including false-positive/false-negative findings and variance by genre and audience.

**Scope:** specified_not_certified. Requires editorial or canonical graph review.

### N120 — Receipt-Governed v8 Release

Freeze a versioned program/manifest, enumerate actual executable checks, run negative and regression tests, preserve v4–v7 behavior, and forbid `shipped` without inspectable command/result/readback receipt.

**Scope:** partially_executable. locally testable manifest and receipts (not upstream shipped)
