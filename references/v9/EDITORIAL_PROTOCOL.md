# Writing Intelligence v9 — Editorial Operating Protocol

**Project author:** Antonio T. Smith Jr.  
**Organization:** Density6 LLC  
**State:** WI9 experimental design; not a v9 release  
**Priority:** Evidence before voice; ideas before ornament; authorial judgment before distribution.

## Purpose

A high-performing writing system must produce more than fluent, well-balanced sentences. It must preserve particular thought, know the difference between a lived event and a hypothetical scene, and respect authorship rules in every publication venue. It must not promise any detector result or conceal substantive AI drafting.

This protocol adds *editorial* tests to the existing v3 craft passes and v4–v8 evidence, semantic, and publication laws. It does not replace those laws.

## Diagnosis of the miniature-world writing benchmark

The benchmark answered the journalist's three questions but exposed defects:

| Defect | What happened | Required remedy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Abstract first-person experience | The writer's claimed miniature-making experience never entered a particular witnessed event | Capture a genuinely author-supplied story, or state that one was not obtained |
| Composite specificity | Hypothetical solar communities and miniature towns risked resembling personal case studies | Label each example as hypothetical or remove it |
| Repetitious binary reframing | Multiple "not X; instead Y" conclusions repeated similar ideas | Test the mechanism, then state only the meaningful distinction |
| Formulaic structure | Three long, parallel answers had comparable transitions and endings | Let the actual facts dictate the length and arrangement of each answer |
| Polished but ownerless thought | Claims could have been given by almost any expert | Find a source-anchored observation, decision, disagreement or correction unique to this writer |
| Surface-first cadence | The craft pack prescribed sentence-length manipulation tied to detector behavior | Restore ordinary editing by comprehension, intent, and author voice; no manufactured randomness |
| Journalism mismatch | A 1,400-word mini essay was not a concise source quote | Build a research/interview brief, then comply with actual editorial format and authorship policy |

A third-party detector's 68% report is a *user-observed classifier output*, not a calibrated probability of AI authorship. Its score must never be used as the optimization target.

## v9 editorial gates (owner review required)

**E01 — Venue contract.** Record the outlet, audience, word limit, genre, publication rules and AI-use policy. `human_only` => prohibit generated or substantively AI-rewritten submission copy.

**E02 — Source custody.** Preserve dated human notes, interview statements, prior writing, original draft and cited research separately. A model's memory of a claim does not turn it into an author observation.

**E03 — Thought inventory.** For every paragraph, identify the actual underlying proposition. If it contains no new distinction, action, evidence, contradiction, or consequence, compress or cut it.

**E04 — Specificity proof.** A detail needs an evidence anchor. Record what the author actually saw, measured, decided or corrected; never fill missing specifics by imagination in nonfiction.

**E05 — Distinguish possible from performed.** A hypothetical house, prototype, experimental town or proposed design remains a hypothetical unless the author supplies a dated source that supports completion.

**E06 — Causality.** Ask which choice changed the outcome. Where causality is unestablished, report observation or hypothesis instead of implying proof.

**E07 — Disagreement.** Identify at least one substantial plausible alternative explanation. A false straw man earns no score.

**E08 — Uncertainty.** Protect legitimate "I do not know." Do not transform honest uncertainty into confident generalization merely because it reads better.

**E09 — Voice stewardship.** Compare to consented authentic author texts from relevant genres. Do not copy distinctive private phrases into another person's work without permission.

**E10 — Meaning-led editing.** Fix repetitive openings, parallel closings, over-explaining, generic slogans and robotic cadence *because they weaken communication*, not because of detector instructions. Do not implant mistakes.

**E11 — Quotability and utility.** For a media pitch, test whether a journalist could actually use the factual insight. For a novel, whether a scene produces change. For a report, whether a decision follows.

**E12 — Trace.** Every substantive addition, deletion or strengthened claim identifies its source and what changed. The author accepts or rejects the proposed meaning change.

**E13 — Release.** A mechanical clean report is insufficient. Require authorization, evidence review, venue-policy compliance, and human judgment. Any unresolved condition => `HOLD`.

## A grounded author interview, not an invented anecdote

The following prompts elicit material; their answers must originate with the writer, not be completed by a model.

- Name the actual miniature, physical model, digital environment or scale simulation you built.
- Identify its original purpose. What decision could the model illuminate?
- What was one concrete limitation? Was something too large, too expensive, difficult to align, or inconsistent?
- Describe a decision you changed because you saw the model.
- What evidence remains: photographs, files, dated notes, diagrams, drawings, iteration history, or a person who observed it?
- What still does not work? Which apparently good idea failed?
- What conclusion do you disagree with in the journalist's premise?
- What observation would make you change your mind?

If the writer cannot provide a particular case, the prose must remain at the level of honest, general reasoning. A company division list is not an incident log.

## Editing review questions

Ask the editor to answer without seeing the author name:

1. Which statement here could only have come from this author's experience or original reasoning?
2. Can every first-person event be traced to the author's own evidence?
3. What is the strongest argument against the thesis, and is it represented fairly?
4. Is there a passage that says the same thing three times?
5. Where has metaphor replaced concrete reasoning?
6. Where is the piece unnecessarily symmetrical?
7. Which detail is memorable because it is *true*, rather than merely vivid?
8. Would a subject-matter expert challenge the scope or implied certainty?
9. Did editing preserve the author's actual position?
10. Does the venue permit this particular authorship and editorial process?

## Style diagnostics: permitted vs prohibited

| Permitted | Prohibited |
| --- | --- |
| Comparing original human samples to edits for meaning and voice fidelity | Claiming style similarity proves the text was human-authored |
| Deleting repeated abstraction | Inserting deliberate typos or "human imperfections" |
| Revising sentence rhythm for reader comprehension | Alternating sentence lengths by formula to game a detector |
| Making scenes specific from author notes | Inventing a memory or composite firsthand scene |
| Keeping a meaningful unconventional phrase an editor can justify | Fabricating idiosyncrasy to simulate a human signature |
| Declaring AI assistance when required | Calling AI-generated text human-authored because someone clicked Send |

## Workflow for human-only outlets

- Accept the query and inspect its restrictions.
- Collect the author’s original experience and sources.
- Prepare research findings, questions, and an issue map without an AI-authored submission draft.
- The author writes their own response independently.
- Evaluate venue permissions again before any editing; if even AI-assisted rewriting is prohibited, do not run it.
- The author alone decides what to submit. Automated release remains disabled.

## Success criteria

A v9 program candidate succeeds when human author review and blind editorial review show that it preserved actual thought and evidence better than the v8 baseline **without** increased factual distortion, invented events, consent failures, or policy violations. No detector score can substitute for this result.
