# Fiction Arc and Ending Addendum — proposed for v8

The conscious external want and the inferred inner need are two distinguishable pressures. They may reinforce, conflict, change or remain unresolved. The author declares a character interpretation; readers may disagree. A four-quadrant ending frame is useful only when the writer intends a terminal binary want/need resolution, and even then it is just one analytical view.

**Human before function:** a narrator, hero, foil, liar, confidant, or antagonist must be more than the structural job they perform. Record relationships, particular needs, constraints, chosen consequences and plausible contradictions without treating them as permanent psychometric labels.

**Different arcs legitimately exist:** positive change grows from choices and observed consequences; flat arcs preserve a central conviction while testing its effect on the world; corruption/fall can compound through decisions and unaddressed beliefs; disillusionment can be stable and unsentimental; open/custom arcs need no artificial moment of revelation.

**Final scenes must do work without announcing it:** a return to an opening object, line, choice or relationship can expose changed meaning. An ironic external victory can carry interior defeat. A sacrifice needs a plausible alternative; a “new self” speech is not evidence of altered conduct. These are creative review questions, not hardcoded grammar rules.

No universal screenplay page count, exact midpoint timestamp, beat-percentage schedule or one obligatory trauma origin is binding in this addendum. An authored deviation is not automatically a defect. All prior fiction and thrillers requirements B001–B060 remain in the separate prior package and must be integrated by one canonical owner later.
