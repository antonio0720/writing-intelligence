# Genre Pack: Academic Writing

## Weighting Adjustments

| Category | Weight Modifier | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence Discipline | +5 | Claims without evidence fail peer review and rubrics |
| Argument Strength | +5 | The thesis must survive scrutiny |
| Clarity | +3 | Complexity of thought ≠ complexity of expression |
| Structural Control | +3 | Each section must perform a declared function |
| Memorability | -5 | Academic writing earns respect through rigor, not quotability |
| Rhythm | -3 | Clarity over musicality; but monotony still penalized |

## Architecture

### Standard Essay
1. Introduction: Hook → Context → Thesis → Roadmap (optional for shorter papers)
2. Body sections: Each organized as Claim → Evidence → Interpretation → Transition
3. Counterargument section: Steelman the opposition → Concede what's true → Pivot to why your position holds
4. Conclusion: Synthesis → Implication → Earned question

### Research Paper
1. Abstract: Problem → Method → Key Finding → Significance (one paragraph)
2. Introduction: Research gap → Research question → Significance → Thesis
3. Literature Review: Organized thematically, not chronologically
4. Method: Approach → Justification → Limitations
5. Results/Analysis: Data organized by sub-question or theme
6. Discussion: Interpretation → Comparison to existing literature → Limitations → Implications
7. Conclusion: Summary finding → Broader significance → Future directions

## Voice Notes

- Confidence comes from evidence, not from declarative force
- Qualify claims honestly: "suggests" when the evidence suggests, "demonstrates" when it demonstrates
- Use discipline-specific terminology precisely — define on first use for mixed audiences
- Avoid first person unless the discipline/professor permits it
- Signal phrases vary: "argues," "demonstrates," "contends," "observes," "notes" — each carries different weight

## Authentic Authorship in Academic Writing

Academic work is where authorship carries the most consequence, so this pack
never helps disguise how a paper came to exist. It does not simulate classroom
attendance, plant artificial imperfections, or promise that a draft will pass an
AI detector (see `references/v4/NON_GOALS.md`). It does the opposite: it makes
the student's own thinking visible and checkable.

1. **Use the course's own language, from the student's materials.** Terms from lectures, assigned readings and discussions come from notes the author supplies. If none were supplied, ask for them; never invent them.
2. **Reference assigned texts by author and argument, not just title**, and only texts the author has read and supplied.
3. **Observations from class come from the author.** An insight that could only come from attending must be one the author actually had and provided. A fabricated observation is a false claim of authorship and is refused.
4. **Engage with specific arguments from sources, not just their conclusions**, each anchored to a span of a supplied source or marked `needs_source`.
5. **State real uncertainty honestly.** Qualify where the evidence is genuinely uncertain. Do not manufacture doubt, rough transitions or errors to look human; that is detector evasion, not craft.
6. **Vary evidence integration on purpose**: paraphrase, short quotation, and longer quotation with analysis, each chosen because it serves the argument.
7. **Follow the institution's AI-use policy.** Where a course, supervisor, funder or publisher requires disclosure of assistance, draft the disclosure with the author. Never suppress or minimize it.

## Common Failures

- Thesis that states a topic instead of a position
- Body paragraphs that are evidence dumps without interpretation
- Counterargument that is strawmanned or absent
- Conclusion that restates the introduction verbatim
- Citations clustered in one section, absent in others
- Block quotes without preceding introduction or following analysis
- "Since the dawn of time" or any universal historical framing

## v9: authorship, sources and venue

*Proposed v9 preview (PR #8), not a release. Venue rules: check the venue's current written policy; unknown means HOLD.*

- **Venue:** journals, theses and course work set their own rules on AI assistance, disclosure and data statements. Read the current written policy; a missing policy is HOLD, not permission.
- **Anchored sources:** every cited finding, effect size, quotation and page reference needs an exact span from the source actually read. A claim remembered from a model is not a citation.
- **Firsthand material:** the author's own data, protocol, lab or field notes, and coding decisions. Planned or hypothetical analyses stay in the future tense and cannot support a "we found" sentence.
- **Disclosure:** commonly required for any generated text or analysis, and sometimes barred for authorship of the manuscript itself. Record the contribution log truthfully.
- **Phrase matches that may be legitimate:** `qualifier_padding` ("it appears that", "may suggest") is often honest calibration of evidence; `staged_explanation` ("this paper argues") is a genre convention. Keep them where the evidence is genuinely uncertain.
