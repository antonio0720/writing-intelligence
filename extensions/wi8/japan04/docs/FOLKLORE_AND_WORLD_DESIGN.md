# Folklore Atlas and optional fiction systems

## Folklore atlas

A `variant` record has its own identity, subject, account, scope, source links, status, and optional ancestor variants. Preserving two incompatible accounts is correct where local traditions differ. Missing earliest evidence is `unknown`, not a guessed historical year. Include tsukumogami, onryō/yūrei, tengu, oni, kitsune, regional legends, modern urban legends and social-historical context *only when specific local sources warrant an entry*. Monster-encounter ethics may include negotiation, coexistence and mutual repair, not just combat.

## What Ki-Threads are

The uploaded author notes specify an invented setting involving a Mundane/Liminal/Hidden structure, object spirits, Ki-Threads, vows, contracts, artifacts, costs, D–S ranks and counterplay. Those mechanics belong to the proposed seed series that chooses them. The 97-page draft sometimes treats cost and counters as absolute laws, but the v8 instruction is more precise: **they are optional per-world commitments**, mutable through deliberate author-approved retcons. No school mystery or ordinary romance needs a magic system. No per-chapter culture quota.

Per-world contracts: allowed capacities, forbidden acts, acquisition, limits, costs, information asymmetry, counterplay, consent, event-time budgets, cumulative debt and exceptions. The example `simulate_power_ledger` proves only that arithmetic matches user-supplied resource events. It does not prove a story's magic or physical realism.

## Six optional source seeds

The source draft's six starters—Neon Shrine Couriers, Tsukumogami Detectives, Sapporo Ice Circuit, Kansai Heist Institute, Okinawa Tidebound and Mecha Tea Club—are documented as **unadopted ideas**. They are not asserted to have been written or accepted by the young author. They must never seed a newly initialized project automatically; promotion requires a separate decision by the creator.

## Genres as lenses

Shōnen/shōjo/seinen/josei describe broad publishing audiences, not fixed narrative genres. Thriller, school slice-of-life, romance, mecha, tournament, mystery, and literary realism combine freely. Mono no aware, ma, wabi-sabi and related aesthetic terms may be studied with provenance and historical nuance, not introduced as compulsory decorative markers. An author can write a quiet single-day story about friendship with no battle, festival, villain or message.
