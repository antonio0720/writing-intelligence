# Multi-Level Goals, Mixed Victories and Bitemporal Reinterpretation

**Normative scope:** C168–C177. The goal graph is hierarchical but not a scalar score. A scene win can secure its immediate objective and simultaneously sacrifice trust, deplete ammunition or increase an adversary's influence. The local and global outcomes are distinct components of an outcome vector. Define narrative improvement by changes in causal opportunity, not by labeling each beat simply yes/no.

A reinterpretation does not rewrite decision-time truth. `interpretations[]` are observations or judgments by named agents at `known_at` instants. `as-of` filters those records to what the observer *was recorded as knowing*, while retaining older entries when later ones differ. Event order in the fictional world and knowledge time in the authoring workspace are different axes.

A major twist lists earlier necessary events; an opponent's guaranteed result requires counterfactual alternatives. A substitute goal names its predecessor and the causal event that made the old target no longer controlling. Dangling substitutions or goal cycles must be rejected. This suite has no proof of thematic payoff absent manuscript evidence and readers.
