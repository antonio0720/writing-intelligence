# Domain Contracts and Failure Semantics

## A. Opening C121–C131

An opening can declare any subset of genre, character, scene and time methods through `strategies`. The list is a repertoire, not a quota. `quiet: true` is never mechanically disallowed. `spectacle: true` without any `causal_anchor_ids` raises a **review**, never a structural failure. `promises[]` record a dimension and a payoff scene or declared deferral. `clues[]` carry exposure scene, reveal scene, and a `retroactively_inserted` indicator; fabricated after-the-fact placement is a structural defect in the *authored projection*. `misdirections[]` distinguish character belief, misleading image, unreliable speaker and direct authorial falsehood. `nonlinear` requires an explicit `disclosure_contract` for review.

**Where this is incomplete:** no manuscript source-span resolver, salience measurement, reader orientation instrument, tone-inversion prediction or genre-promise editorial judge. These require upstream ownership and human calibration.

## B. Action C132–C145

`spaces[]` declare topological nodes, permitted edges, sight lines. `fights[].actors[]` declare starting position, objective and resource stock. `beats[]` carry movement target, known resource deltas, claimed observers and optional setup and adaptation references. Impossible direct moves, observation across undeclared sight lines, resources below zero and unknown actors are structural findings under the declared topology. A teleport or traversal exception must identify the canon mechanism; the current CLI **checks only that a reference is declared**, not whether it truly exists in canon. That unresolved verification belongs to the upstream graph adapter.

The Set–Rise–Twist–Tie technique from notes is an **optional** orchestration. No number or order of beats is required. Equal-density blow-by-blow coverage is not assumed. A fight with no `resulting_changes` is an editorial review prompt, not proof of bad fiction.

## C. Opposition C146–C158

`forces[]` can denote people, groups, institutions, environment or inner contradiction. Archetype labels may be multiple or absent. Sympathetic motives never negate `harm_refs`. `plans[].steps[].choice_dependencies[]` distinguish independent human choices from controlled dependencies. A critical step predicated on one uncontrollable decision without contingency receives `WI8-C03-OMNISCIENT-PLAN`. A claimed robust plan without alternatives receives `WI8-C03-UNTESTED-PLAN`.

**Unimplemented:** reliable witness epistemics, private-vs-public motive inference, mentor-mask reveal assessment and moral profile scoring. Those are documented interpretation contracts, not executable results.

## D. Suspense and speech C159–C167

`pledges[]` tie a raised question or promised reveal to a payoff scene and payoff evidence or deliberate carry. A mere scene pointer without evidence is not payoff. `beats[]` preserve `reader_knows`, `actor_knowledge` and `used_knowledge`; facts acted on but never declared known are structural defects in the projection. `clocks[]` require an expiry condition and a declared choice consequence. `dialogue[]` can represent persuasion, probing or power shifts in speech with tactics and state change. A static conversation is flagged for review, but may still be beautiful.

**Unimplemented:** perceived hazard probabilities and real-reader forecasts. Those cannot be machine certified from annotations.

## E. Sequences C168–C177

`goals[]` form an acyclic tree/forest with novel→act→sequence→scene semantics. Unknown parent and cycle block. A replacement goal needs an explicit `replaces_id` and `cause_event`. `outcomes[]` carry simultaneous `local_result`, `resource_cost`, `relationship_cost` and `antagonist_benefit`: a locally achieved goal can benefit the adversary. `interpretations[]` preserve each observer's time-aware opinion; late discoveries add later rows. The CLI's `as-of` query never overwrites the early record. Major `twists[]` name required world events and optional counterfactuals; no test result here proves the plot will satisfy actual readers.

## F. Source and medium C178–C180

`references[]` label real-world facts independently from fictional or interpretive ideas. Real-world entries need source URI, effective date, check date and rights status; `claims_current` also requires an explicit freshness policy. The analyzer does **not** browse to verify them. `adaptations[]` associate source/target scene, medium and meaning identifiers; a mismatch raises review and does not force identical presentation conventions. Cultural portrayal should use consented human review and rights-aware sourcing beyond these structural checks.

## Finding categories

Every finding carries `severity`, `code`, `dimension`, `record`, `requirement`. Severity `error` means the typed projection is inconsistent (relative to its declarations); `review` means a human should assess it. Empty findings mean *no defined detector fired*, not literary success. A semantic/canon change must re-run the projection against the new canonical state and invalidate stale review evidence.
