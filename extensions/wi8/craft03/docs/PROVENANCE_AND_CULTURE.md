# Provenance, Attribution, Culture and Media Boundaries

`SOURCE_SPECIFICATION.md` came from the user-supplied C121–C180 narrative expansion. Its citations to a 51-page *The Craft* PDF are part of that text; this task did not receive the underlying PDF. They are therefore *asserted citation metadata* and not page-verified evidence. This independent implementation uses newly written contracts, original sample characters and no reproduced third-party scripts or film images.

Writing about living cultures, histories, skin tone, accent, folklore, policing, military practice or technology requires separate rights-aware factual investigation appropriate to story period, location and intended audience. No stock metaphor catalog counts as cultural verification. An author's invention may be canon; it does not become real-world fact. An in-world narrator may lie; that does not license the tool to invent outside evidence.

If scientific, tactical or procedural claims appear in external-facing text, record source, authoritative publisher, stated effective date, observation/check date, intended application, uncertainty and use rights. A cited old notebook item about an agency's historical practice does not attest to current law, policy or operational procedure.

For script/novel/audio/comic/game adaptation, retain meaning identities (crucial decision, consequence, information reveal) while recomputing how they are expressed. Script sound formatting, script page/minute assumptions and shot vocabulary are optional craft vocabulary in a novel, not mandatory format law.

Human beta reading: consent to retain feedback, anonymization strategy, question script, audience inclusion criteria, sample size, intended audience, analysis method, disagreement and author response should be written to a signed review record. This module does not gather respondents and cannot claim reader impact.
